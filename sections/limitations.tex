\paragraph{Model scope.}
We evaluate with three verifiers (LLaMA-8B, LLaMA-70B, Gemma-4B) and two Mamba2 drafter sizes (27M, 45M backbone).
Generalization to other verifier families (e.g., Mistral, Qwen) and alternative SSM architectures (e.g., RWKV, linear attention) remains to be validated.

\paragraph{GPU-only speedup.}
On a fast GPU (H100 NVL), speculative decoding with our tiny drafter yields throughput below the autoregressive baseline ($0.49\times$ for guided Mamba2-27M, $0.83\times$ for LLaMA-1B).
The real-world speedup materializes in the CPU-offloaded pipeline; the projected $2.7{\times}$ gain assumes perfect pipeline overlap and has not yet been measured in a fully asynchronous end-to-end implementation.

\paragraph{45M drafter pretraining.}
The Mamba2-45M pretrained checkpoint was undertrained (0.38 epochs on FineWeb-Edu vs.\ $\sim$1 epoch for the 27M variant).
Its pretrained baseline (1.52) is lower than the 27M's (1.86), making the 45M guidance gains partially confounded with the KD/guidance training compensating for insufficient pretraining.
A properly pretrained 45M backbone may yield even higher guided acceptance.

\paragraph{Training data distribution.}
The guided drafter was trained with knowledge distillation from the verifier on UltraChat data.
Domain shift between training and evaluation benchmarks may affect acceptance rates, particularly for specialized domains like code (HumanEval) and math (GSM8K), where Gemma-4B acceptance is notably low.

\paragraph{Broader impact.}
Speculative decoding accelerates text generation, which could amplify both beneficial and harmful uses of language models.
Our work does not introduce new capabilities beyond speed; all outputs are provably identical to the verifier's distribution.
