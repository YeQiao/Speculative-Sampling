Speculative decoding accelerates large language model (LLM) inference by drafting candidate tokens with a fast model and verifying them in parallel with the target model.
Recent self-drafting methods (Medusa heads, early exits, self-speculation) avoid a separate drafter by reusing verifier components, but they consume additional GPU memory, require modifying or retraining the verifier, and offer limited real-time latency gains when the verification pass itself dominates wall-clock time.
They also degrade when inputs are out of distribution, because their drafting quality is tightly coupled to the verifier's internal features.
External pretrained drafters, by contrast, retain stronger standalone generation capabilities and higher acceptance rates, yet existing designs rely on transformer architectures that carry quadratic attention costs and large KV-cache overhead.

We present \textbf{SpecSSM}, the first framework to combine an \emph{externally pretrained} state space model drafter with verifier-guided injection and full CPU offloading.
Our drafter is a Mamba2 model with only 27M backbone parameters ($44\times$ smaller than LLaMA-3.2-1B) that receives hidden-state signals from the verifier via lightweight linear projections into Mamba2's state-update branch, enabling the recurrent drafter to closely approximate the verifier's distribution without any verifier modification.
We further propose \emph{activation replay}, an efficient method to resynchronize the SSM's recurrent state after speculative rejection in $O(K)$ time rather than $O(T)$ full re-prefill, yielding up to $201\times$ speedup on CPU at 1024-token contexts.
Motivated by our own bottleneck analysis showing that linear projections account for 85\% of SSM inference time, we implement a CPU-offloaded pipeline with a custom AVX-512/INT8 kernel tailored to these layers, completing 8-token drafts in 9\,ms, fitting entirely within the GPU verification window for asynchronous overlap.
Across five diverse benchmarks with LLaMA-3.1-8B as verifier, our guided Mamba2 drafter achieves 2.33 mean accepted tokens per round (with correct rejection masking), a 25\% improvement over the unguided pretrained baseline (1.86).
Guidance scales with verifier size: acceptance rises to 2.51 with LLaMA-3.1-70B.
Our projected CPU-GPU pipeline delivers $2.7\times$ throughput over autoregressive decoding while freeing all GPU memory for the verifier.
