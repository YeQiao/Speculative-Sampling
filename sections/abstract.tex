Speculative decoding accelerates large language model inference by first drafting candidate tokens with a lightweight, fast model and then verifying them in parallel with the target model.
Existing self-drafting methods can reduce training complexity and reuse verifier components, but consume substantial GPU memory, require verifier modifications, and struggle with out-of-distribution data; meanwhile external transformer-based drafters are more resilient but still suffer from quadratic attention costs.
We present \textbf{SpecSSM}, which combines an externally pretrained Mamba2 drafter (27M and 45M backbone parameters, $44\times$ and $22\times$ smaller than LLaMA-3.2-1B) with a verifier-guided hidden-state injection and offloads the entire drafter computation to the CPU.
Lightweight projection layers steer the recurrent drafter toward the verifier's distribution without modifying the verifier itself.
Our \emph{activation replay} mechanism resynchronizes the SSM state after a rejected draft in time proportional to the number of rejected tokens $O(K)$ instead of the full sequence length $O(T)$, yielding up to a $201\times$ speedup over a full re-prefill.
A custom AVX-512/INT8 CPU kernel targets the linear-projection bottleneck that dominates 85\% of SSM inference cost, enabling 8-token drafts to be produced in only 9\,ms, well within the GPU verification window.
Across five independent benchmarks using LLaMA-3.1-8B, the guided Mamba2-45M drafter averages 2.82 accepted tokens per round (+85\% over the pretrained baseline), while the 27M variant averages 2.33 (+25\%).
