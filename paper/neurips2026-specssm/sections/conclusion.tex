We presented SpecSSM, a speculative decoding framework that pairs a tiny Mamba2-27M drafter (27M backbone parameters) with transformer verifiers of varying scale.
By injecting verifier hidden states into the SSM's state-update pathway, our guided drafter achieves a mean of 2.33 accepted tokens per speculation round across five diverse benchmarks with LLaMA-8B, a 25\% improvement over the unguided baseline.
With LLaMA-70B, pretrained acceptance rises to 2.51 (+35\% over 8B), demonstrating that guidance scales with verifier quality.

We introduced activation replay as an efficient method to resynchronize the SSM's recurrent state after speculative rejection, replacing $O(T)$ re-prefill with $O(K)$ replay.
On CPU, this yields up to $201{\times}$ speedup at 1024-token contexts, making it essential for the CPU-offloaded pipeline.
Our custom AVX-512/INT8 kernel stack achieves $7.5{\times}$ over na\"ive PyTorch, completing 8-token drafts in 9\,ms, well within the 16\,ms GPU verification window for asynchronous overlap.
The projected pipeline throughput of 202\,tok/s represents a $2.7{\times}$ improvement over autoregressive decoding.

Our masking analysis documented a previously unreported asymmetry between recurrent and transformer drafters under non-compact speculative layouts, underscoring the importance of correct evaluation methodology for SSM-based systems.

\paragraph{Future work.}
(1)~Implement and benchmark the full asynchronous CPU-GPU pipeline;
(2)~context length scaling experiments to quantify replay benefits over longer generations;
(3)~tree-structured drafting to further improve acceptance;
(4)~scaling guided SSM drafters to 130M+ parameters.
