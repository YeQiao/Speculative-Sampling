\paragraph{Model scope.}
We evaluate with three verifiers (LLaMA-8B, LLaMA-70B, Gemma-4B) and one primary drafter size (Mamba2-27M).
Generalization to other verifier families (e.g., Mistral, Qwen) and larger SSM drafters remains to be validated.

\paragraph{GPU-only speedup.}
On a fast GPU (H100 NVL), speculative decoding with our tiny drafter yields throughput below the autoregressive baseline (0.43$\times$ for guided Mamba2, 0.83$\times$ for LLaMA-1B).
The real-world speedup materializes in the CPU-offloaded pipeline; the projected $2.7{\times}$ gain has not yet been measured end-to-end in a fully asynchronous implementation.

\paragraph{Training data distribution.}
The guided drafter was trained with knowledge distillation from the verifier on UltraChat data.
Domain shift between training and evaluation benchmarks may affect acceptance rates, particularly for specialized domains like code (HumanEval) and math (GSM8K).

\paragraph{Broader impact.}
Speculative decoding accelerates text generation, which could amplify both beneficial and harmful uses of language models.
Our work does not introduce new capabilities beyond speed; all outputs are provably identical to the verifier's distribution.
