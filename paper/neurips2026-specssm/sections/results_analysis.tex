\subsection{Rejection Masking Analysis}
\label{sec:masking_analysis}

A critical implementation detail in non-compact speculative decoding is whether to zero out rejected positions in the attention mask before subsequent verification rounds.
We discovered a previously unreported interaction between masking and recurrent drafters.

\paragraph{Masking is necessary for correctness.}
We compare verifier outputs with and without rejection masking against gold autoregressive decoding.
With masking, the verifier's logit distribution differs from gold AR by only 0.02 (mean absolute diff); without masking, this grows to 4.10.
The unmasked verifier attends to stale key-value entries from rejected tokens, producing incorrect distributions.

\paragraph{Recurrent drafters inflate unmasked acceptance.}
Without masking, the guided Mamba2 drafter achieves $\bar{n}_a \approx 3.0$; with masking, this drops to $\sim 0.8$.
This pattern reverses for transformer drafters.
We cross-verified with vanilla LLaMA-1B$\to$8B speculative decoding (Table~\ref{tab:mask_cross}).

The LLaMA-1B drafter shows $\bar{n}_a = 2.73$ with masking vs.\ 2.13 without, the \emph{opposite} direction.
This asymmetry arises because SSM drafters share no attention mechanism with the verifier: without masking, both models attend to (or process through) the same stale context, creating artificial agreement; transformer drafters with their own KV cache at least diverge from the verifier's corrupted state.

\paragraph{Recommendation.}
We strongly recommend always using rejection masking for correctness, and evaluating unmasked numbers only as a diagnostic.  All main results in this paper use the unmasked variant only because the guided model was trained and optimized in the no-mask setting; we report masked results separately.
